# Aesthetic Rubric

Use this rubric after the script-based first pass. The scanner can flag risks, but final selection is an editorial decision.

## Editing Actions, Not Just Scores

Visual judgment should become a sequence of review actions:

- find 1-3 possible standout photos in every scene
- compare near-duplicates side by side
- rescue strong single images from weak scenes
- use final contact sheets to catch semantic repetition
- inspect near misses before delivery
- when the user names a liked image, expand from that visual preference seed
- for portraits, inspect face/body crops before finalizing

Do not ask the model to trust a global score. Ask it to compare, reject its own first pass, and revise.

## Universal Order Of Judgment

1. Moment: Is there a decisive expression, gesture, action, or relationship?
2. Subject: Is the viewer's attention clear?
3. Light: Does the light create shape, mood, or useful information?
4. Composition: Are edges, spacing, depth, and visual weight controlled?
5. Story: Does the image say something that another image in the set does not?
6. Technical fitness: Is sharpness, exposure, color, and resolution good enough for the intended use?
7. Edit potential: Can a crop, exposure adjustment, or black-and-white treatment rescue a strong image?

Do not let technical scores dominate the first five items. A slightly soft image with the best moment can beat a perfect but empty frame.

## Duplicate Group Selection

Within a near-duplicate group, pick the image with:

- the strongest expression or gesture
- the cleanest subject separation
- the fewest edge distractions
- the best body posture and hand position
- the most natural eye line
- enough sharpness for the intended output

If two frames are close, keep one and mark the other REVIEW only when it has a genuinely different use.

## Portraits

Prefer:

- alive eyes and natural expression
- relaxed jaw, hands, and shoulders
- flattering light direction
- clean background around the head
- posture that matches the subject's personality

Watch for:

- half blinks
- mouth shapes between expressions
- awkward hands
- tangent lines through the head
- focus on ears, hair, or background instead of the eyes
- technically good faces that feel stiff, unflattering, or unlike the subject

For people-heavy selections, always compare the selected frame against nearby alternatives before final delivery. The best technical frame is often not the best face frame.

Do a final face tuning pass whenever the selected set contains people. This is not a generic beauty score; it is a frame-choice pass for expression, timing, angle, posture, and whether the subject would feel fairly represented.

## Events And Documentary

Prefer:

- key people and key actions
- images that explain what happened
- interaction between people
- readable context in the environment
- sequence coverage: wide, medium, close, detail

Do not reject an imperfect frame if it captures the only important moment.

## Travel, Street, And Lifestyle

Prefer:

- layered foreground, subject, and background
- light that creates place and time
- clean geometry or intentional visual tension
- human scale
- images that add variety to the story

Slight motion blur, shadow, silhouettes, and imperfect framing can be acceptable when they add atmosphere.

## Product, Food, And Object Photos

Prefer:

- clear hero angle
- readable shape and texture
- clean edges
- controlled highlights
- color that feels appetizing or accurate
- enough negative space for layout use when needed

Reject obvious duplicates aggressively unless there is a meaningful angle, detail, or crop difference.

## Red Flags That Can Still Be Keepers

Keep reviewing instead of auto-rejecting when:

- blur is intentional or expressive
- exposure is low-key or high-key by design
- the photo is the only record of a key moment
- the frame has strong emotion despite technical flaws
- cropping can remove the main distraction

## Final Selection Discipline

Every KEEP should have a role:

- hero image
- alternate crop or mood
- required person/object
- story bridge
- detail shot
- client-safe backup

If the role is unclear, mark REVIEW or UNSELECTED.

Use `UNSELECTED` when an image is usable but not part of the current final set. Use `REJECT` only for clearly unusable images.
